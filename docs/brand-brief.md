# Creative brief — Birta wordmark & logo identity

Durable creative direction for the Birta visual identity: the wordmarks and logo. Written
to brief a design effort and to stay useful as context at any later stage. The full
reasoning lives in [`docs/BRAND.md`](BRAND.md); this is the distilled brief.

---

## What Birta is

**Birta Labs** is a small software studio. Its first product, **Birta Writer**, is a
WYSIWYG Markdown editor for VS Code — you edit the real `.md` file in place, formatted, so
what you see is what's on disk. More products will follow. The audience sits between
developer tools and consumer software: technical, taste-driven, allergic to hype.

**The name is the concept.** *Birta* is Icelandic for the **quality of light** — the
ambient glow in a room, not a lamp — and the verb **to publish / reveal / bring to light**
(*birting* = a publication, and also daybreak). That is the work in one word: the raw
source *brought into the light and shown plainly*. Render this; don't invent a new concept.

## The system

The identity is **the word, drawn** — never a symbol standing in for it. Three tiers:

- **Birta** — one reusable wordmark, the constant across every product.
- **Birta [Product]** — each product word carries its own distinctive typography and
  personality, strong enough to **stand alone** with no "Birta" beside it.
- **Birta Labs** — the company name; used rarely, "Labs" set plainly.

A standard lockup pairs "Birta" with a product word where the connection needs showing
(first contact, app icons). So the identity lives at more than one scale at once: the
standalone word, the paired lockup, and — because a wordmark has no tiny form — a single
utility glyph for favicon-scale contexts (a functional device, not a logo).

## The character

- Birta is **warm, humanist, plain-spoken** — honest and unfussy, never cold or clinical.
  A product word may run cooler or more structured if its product calls for it; Birta
  itself carries the warmth.
- Where the meaning shows, it shows as **light rendered through value and negative space**
  — how light falls, the space it fills — living quietly inside the letterforms, never as a
  depicted light.
- **Plainness is a virtue, not a fallback.** Between two strong options, the plainer wins.
- It must hold in **a single colour** and **at small sizes**. The mark earns its keep in
  black on white before anything else.

## Not this

- **No monogram** — Birta is never reduced to a single letter or a symbol. (The utility
  glyph is a functional exception, not a brand mark.)
- **No depicted light source** — no sun, rays, lightbulb, lens-flare, spark.
- **No literal props** — no pens, nibs, documents, pages, cursors.
- **No Nordic kitsch** — no runes, glaciers, aurora, flags. The warmth carries the origin.
- **No gradients, drop shadows, bevels, or glass.**

## Colour

Work in **black and white.** The palette is already settled — inherited from
`harlanlewis.com` (warm neutral grounds; an accent that shifts between light and dark).
Resolved wordmarks drop into it later. If it doesn't work in one value, it isn't done.

## What a strong result does

- **Stands alone** — cover "Birta", and the product word still reads as a distinctive mark.
- **Connects** — the lockup reads as one family, including in a square (app icon), with no
  symbol to lean on.
- **Survives small** — the utility glyph is legible and unmistakable at favicon size, one
  colour, on light or dark.
- **Says itself** — the concept ties back to *birta* in a sentence.
- **Owns itself** — the name is a common word; the distinctiveness lives in the lettering,
  and that is what's protectable.
